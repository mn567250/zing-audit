One chart, linear, readable probe

Every variant below uses the same chart grammar, built from your feedback. Only the way each one makes room for the probe differs. Same synthetic run as round 1. Hover, tap, or focus a chart and use the arrow keys to step through requests.

Kept in every variant

  • One chart with a linear y-axis.
  • One running median per endpoint (last 5 requests, as today) inside its p25–p75 band over the same window.
  • Probe dots are smaller and lighter: r=3, 50% opacity, no ring.

Left out

  • Split panels, log scale, a probe background, and separate medians per phase.

F · Your two likes on the current axis

no rescaling

The band and the lighter probe dots on today's full linear range, with nothing else changed. This shows what those two changes do alone.

Gains

  • The probe no longer looks like a chain of rings, and the band shows where the median moves.

Costs

  • The probe still sits in the bottom 2 px, so the vertical squash is unsolved. Each variant below adds one way to fix it.
Option 5

G · Follow the probe

recommended

While there is probe data, the chart shows the probe's time window and fits y to it. The view switch returns to the whole run. Earlier requests are counted at the left edge rather than squeezed in.

Gains

  • Fixes both problems: 100 probe requests spread across the full width, and their 1–3 s spread fills the height.
  • Plain linear axes with zero at the bottom and the familiar time axis.
  • "Whole run" gives exactly today's view with the new marks, so nothing is lost.
  • The switch is a two-button group with a pressed state, so it is reachable by keyboard and screen reader.

Costs

  • The default view changes when the probe starts. It needs one short transition (none with reduced motion) and the switch label must stay visible.
  • The median enters the window still carrying audit values, so its first 4 points come from above. This is honest: it shows the switch from audit to probe.
Option 6

H · Fit y to the latest requests

whole timeline

The x-axis keeps the whole run. The y-axis fits the last 20 requests (1.25 × their p95), so during a probe it fits the probe. Older values above the range become ▲ markers on the top edge with a count, and their exact values stay in the tooltip.

Gains

  • The whole run stays in view, with no switch to learn.
  • Works for every metric and adapts by itself: during the audit phase the axis still covers the audit values.

Costs

  • The probe still covers only about 20% of the width, so dots overlap sideways (the lighter marks help).
  • Most audit points collapse into ▲ markers, and their heights can no longer be compared.
Option 7

I · Broken y-axis

everything visible

Two linear sections on one axis. The lower 65% covers 0 up to the probe's range, and the upper 35% covers the rest up to the slowest audit request. A ≈ mark and a gap with no gridlines show the break.

Gains

  • Every point keeps a real position on a linear scale, and nothing becomes a marker.
  • The probe gets most of the height, and audit values are still comparable to each other.

Costs

  • The distance between the two sections is not to scale. Readers who miss the break misjudge the gap, so the accessible summary must say "axis break at 4 s".
  • Sideways overlap stays, as in H.
  • Lines that cross the break look steeper than they are.

Notes for building it

  1. Band data: RunningMedian in zing/perf/trend.py already holds each endpoint's last-5 window. Adding trend_lo/trend_hi (p25/p75 of that window) next to trend keeps the browser drawing only, as it does today. With 5 values, p25/p75 are the 2nd and 4th smallest.
  2. Probe dots no longer have a ring, so their hit area comes from nearest-point hover (24 px), not the dot. Keyboard stepping and the live region work as in round 1.
  3. Contrast: one translucent dot is fainter than 3:1. The median line (full series colour, 2 px), the tooltip and the data table carry the values. The audit dots keep their outline, so audit (outlined) and probe (small, no outline) differ in shape as well as colour.
  4. Tick labels use readable units ("1.5 s", "120 s") in every variant.
